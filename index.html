Numerical Representation

Embedding Vector Explorer

How do numerical IDs become meaningful concepts? Neural networks map each Token ID into a continuous multi-dimensional vector representing syntactic and semantic features.

Dimension 1 (X)

Lexical complexity, word length, & character density balance.

Dimension 2 (Y)

Syntactic role heuristic (capitalization, punctuation, grammatical endings).

Dimension 3 (Z)

Harmonic character frequency spectrum & phonetic structure.

Dimension 4 (W)

Latent context weight and semantic uniqueness index.

* Note: Educational demo embeddings are 4-dimensional normalized vectors generated deterministically. Real LLMs use 1,536 to 4,096 dimensions.

What am I seeing in this 3D space?

Every floating node represents a token from your text. The 3D coordinates (X, Y, Z) are mathematically mapped from the token's educational embedding vector (Dimension 1 as X, Dimension 2 as Y, Dimension 3 as Z). The glowing coordinate grid lines demonstrate spatial separation.

Why do Vectors matter for AI?

In modern Large Language Models (LLMs), tokens that share similar contexts or grammatical roles gravitate towards nearby regions in vector space. Geometry allows neural networks to perform semantic reasoning via vector algebra!

Foundational Knowledge

How AI Understands Language

A beginner-friendly guide to modern NLP concepts for first-year Science & Artificial Intelligence students.

1. What is Tokenization?

Tokenization is the very first step in Natural Language Processing. It breaks continuous raw strings of human characters into smaller, standardized pieces called tokens.

Example: "Learning AI is fun!" → ["Learning", "AI", "is", "fun", "!"]

2. What are Tokens?

Tokens are the atomic units of text for a language model. They can be whole words, subwords, individual letters, or punctuation marks. Models predict the next token one piece at a time.

Key Concept: Common words are single tokens, while rare words are broken into subwords (e.g., "unbreakable" → "un" + "break" + "able").

3. What are Token IDs?

Computers cannot calculate words—they only do math on numbers. Every unique token in the model's fixed vocabulary dictionary is assigned an integer index called a Token ID.

Lookup Table: "AI" → 104, "robot" → 3409. The exact same token always gets the same ID!

4. What are Embeddings?

An embedding transforms a discrete, arbitrary Token ID into a dense list of real numbers (a vector). These numbers represent geometric positions that encode the token's meaning and syntax.

Vector representation: "king" = [0.91, 0.32, -0.45], "queen" = [0.89, 0.35, 0.62]

5. Why does AI use Embeddings?

Embeddings enable vector arithmetic! In vector space, words with similar meanings cluster together. Neural networks use these coordinates to reason about context, analogies, and grammar.

Famous Equation: Vector("King") - Vector("Man") + Vector("Woman") ≈ Vector("Queen")
